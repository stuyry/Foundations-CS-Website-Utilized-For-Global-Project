Der Giftpilz (The Poisonous Mushroom) (1938)
  • This cartoony Nazi-era book depicts how, like how hard it is to differentiate between poisonous and edible mushrooms, it is also difficult to differentiate between a Jew and a Gentile (non-Jew). It was used to indoctrinate young children during the Third Reich.
  • "Here, kids, I have some candy for you. But you both have to come with me..." This implies that there is a great risk of Jewish people kidnapping kids, using fear mongering to promote a harmful belief against Jewish people. Also note the illustrated stereotype of a Jew’s long crooked nose, which labels them as subhuman and untrustworthy.
  • "The G-d of the Jews is money. To earn money, he commits the greatest crimes. He will not rest until he can sit on a huge money sack, until he has become the king of money.“ This claims the principal value of Jews is wealth, painting them as greedy and willing to play dirty to fulfill any desires. They are painted as greedy and deceitful.
Le Silence de la mer
(Silence of the Sea) (1942)
  • Published secretly in Paris during Germany’s annexation, this novella narrates a story of an old man and his niece showing resistance through silence to a German soldier, himself also unaware of the true motives of the Nazi Party, occupying their house in France during World War II. Vercor’s book became a symbol of French resistance against Germany.
  • “Je ne puis sans souffrir offenser un homme, fût-il mon ennemi.”
  • (“I cannot offend a man, even if he is my enemy, without suffering.”)

A War-Time Handbook for Young Americans (1942)
  • American books like these published during World War II taught children how to be a “good patriotic citizen”: growing gardens, eating all their dinner, collecting scrap metal and rubber, and behaving well for their parents—all for the war effort.
The Moon Is Down (1942)
  • In Steinbeck’s novel, a coastal town is ambushed by an invading army without much struggle. However, unrest quickly grows within the occupied town, and regular attacks against the enemy army occur. It’s clear that the fighting spirit of the town will not die anytime soon.
  • Although fictional, this book was a huge work of propaganda, especially in Europe, where it was secretly translated and smuggled, encouraging resistance movements from real occupied territories during World War II.
