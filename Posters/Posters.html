Europe's Posters
Hinter den Feindmächten: der Jude (1941)
Source Link
(Behind the enemy powers: the Jew)

This poster illustrates a Jewish person slyly watching from behind the flags of the Allied powers: the United States, Great Britain, and the USSR. It aimed to spread the idea that Jewish people were working with and controlling the “enemy powers”, calling to stop them before it was too late.
Don’t Do it Mother (1939-1945)
Source Link
The ghost of Hitler is depicted as tempting a mother to take her children back to the city. The British government made these posters to convince families to move to the countryside and stay there, out of fear of Germany’s air force raiding British major cities, which revealed itself during the Battle of Britain.
Americas's Posters
How to Tell Japs from the Chinese, Life Magazine (1941)
Source Link
To avoid mistaken attacks on Chinese immigrants by American citizens, which at the time were America’s friends, “informative” articles like these were published into newspapers across the country. However, much of this information was incredibly misleading as they relied on stereotypes. Simply put, America fought racism with racism.
Food is a weapon : don't waste it! : buy wisely, cook carefully, eat it all. (1943)
Source Link
The phrase “Food is a weapon” showed how important it was for citizens in the US to ration for the war effort in the Pacific: the extra food saved was one of the only things keeping soldiers from victory--or defeat.
