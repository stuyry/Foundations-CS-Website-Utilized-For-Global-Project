American Songs: The Andrew Sisters!

Introduction
  • Consisted of three members, LaVerne (contralto/low), Maxene (soprano/high), and Patty (mezzo-soprano/lead).
  • Musically, they were heavily influenced by the popular Boswell Sisters at the time, singing in close harmonies, as if to sound like a single voice.
  • Although they had already amassed massive amounts of population before the war, they traveled around the world to perform at war zones, capturing the ‘powerful’ spirit of the draft, as well as boosting morale for many of the soldiers fighting. Songs like “Rum and Coca-Cola” glorified the presence of American GIs in the Caribbean.
Video - Official Sheet Music
  • Using simple major chord progressions in C, with a tempo of 172 BPM, the high-pitched acapella composition had a very positive and natural pulse to it, adding to the theme of propaganda that it encompassed.
  • With an upbeat melody and fast paced beat, it was supposed to insert morale and induce a positive connotation to surround forward momentum during the war, particularly using the theme and message of a band that is happy to join the army.
  • As seen in the music and the sheet music, Lots of swing melodies are present, utilizing the repetition of eighth rests and tied eighth notes in the runs that follow, they are also able to create a very jazzy and catchy tune, mutual to the many ‘propaganda’ songs that developed during the WW2 period.
